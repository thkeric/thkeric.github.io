Ignis Oasis

Visual identity, icon set, poster campaign and a 22-second motion infographic for a speculative climate protest organisation.

My role
Solo: concept, identity, posters, motion, guidelines
Context
Elements of Digital Design, MSc at the University of Edinburgh
When
October to December 2024
Outputs
Logo, icons, five posters, motion piece, guidelines

Overview

Ignis Oasis is a design-fiction brief: a non-violent protest organisation founded in 2015 alongside the Paris Agreement, campaigning in 2030 on rising sea levels and surface temperatures. Before any visual work, I wrote its manifesto around three values, community, awareness and action, and distilled it into a single line: If we burn, you burn with us.

Every deliverable, from the logo to the posters, had to express that line. While not a product design project, it's where I learned to build a visual system and document it so others can apply it consistently.

An identity split in half

The "i" and the "o" are cut through the middle. The top half stands for heatwaves and rising temperatures, the bottom half for rising oceans. For the final version, I recoloured the mark to match the posters and the redesigned icons.

Icons that belong to the posters

Feedback said my first icons felt out of place next to the hyper-real posters. I removed the shields, kept the "i" inside all five icons so they still read as a family, and coloured them with the brand's fire orange (#E87C2D) and water blue (#47ABE5).

Five posters, alone or together

Each poster works on its own, and the five work as a set on a billboard. The main typeface, Gunship, and a secondary one, Futura, are split between them to keep the set varied.

What printing taught me

I'd only ever designed for screens. When I printed the posters in CMYK, the filters on the centre poster lost their glow, because they only existed in RGB. I redesigned it to hold up in print. It was a useful lesson in checking work in the medium people will actually see it in.

A 22-second motion piece

The logo opens into a rotating Earth, and the climate disasters since the Paris Agreement appear around it. The planet then fades to grey behind two charts, sea level and surface temperature, before the piece ends on the slogan. I storyboarded it on paper first, then built it in After Effects. I tried adding sound, but it distracted from the data, so I left it out.

Writing it down

I documented the whole system in brand guidelines, 28 pages in the final version and set in the brand's own typeface. They cover the manifesto, logo sizes and variants, colour, type roles with fallbacks, icons, posters and motion, so someone else could keep the brand consistent without me.

Some photographic elements in the posters and video come from Pexels and Freepik, used under their licences and credited in my project report.
